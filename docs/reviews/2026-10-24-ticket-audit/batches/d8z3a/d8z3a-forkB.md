## Cross-ticket patterns

1. **Audit grades, not failures, drove the work.** Only ct-idhg cites a measurement (548 PUTs/GETs). The rest are static traces of "below B+" functions. Most AC repeat the intended mechanism ("process-local proof tied to exact files…", "enforce event-count, event-byte and total-byte limits") instead of a measurable outcome, so every ticket could be closed by implementing itself. The audit artifacts they cite are no longer retained.
2. **Four bespoke process-local capability registries** exist to avoid re-hashing or re-reading: `VerifiedFile._issued` (`custody.py:40`), `InventoryValidation` `_issued` with code-object ids (`viewer_inventory.py:13`, `28-30`), `GuardedPublicationClient._receipts` (`client.py:57`) and `Continuation._reconciled` (`continuation.py:131`). They cost real complexity, and one later refactor (3089e771) silently opened a forgery hole in the shared base. The batch's net fresh-publication time went up 7.5% (ct-8uwj).
3. **The hand-rolled write path was hardened, not replaced.** The write-ahead journal, intent events and full readback per PUT were bounded and measured (ct-atjy, ct-idhg, ct-1mbc) while docspec-rs already provides create-only remote writes (`objects.rs:533-540`). ct-te1p's planned retirement never happened, and ct-2yl5 added a second, unguarded write route, so there are now two publication write paths.
4. **Same-day siblings duplicated helpers.** ct-hm4b's shared bounded reader was not reused by ct-fcxg's `verify.control`, and the limit tables are duplicated.
