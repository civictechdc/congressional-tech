**Severity ranking (verdicts):**
1. ct-fw0m: forgeable VerifiedFile plus trust across restarts; a regression; high.
2. ct-hnxb: no hosted full-catalog qualification of native C5; raw evidence missing; medium.
3. ct-3djs: closure gap fixed later by ct-u7gg; dormant proof API; low.

**Cross-ticket patterns:**
- **Unmeasured efficiency premise.** The three efficiency tickets (3djs, fw0m, gubr) asserted costs from a static audit whose source is now missing. They were verified only by fixture work counts (29 B, 700 B, 25k rows). The combined result was a fresh publication 7.5% *slower* (ct-8uwj).
- **Bespoke trust layer.** A "process-local proof" layer was copied across 3djs, fw0m (and ct-1dsp) to avoid re-hashing: weakref issuance registries plus inode/ctime/pid plus validator code identity. It is high in complexity for an unmeasured CPU saving and brittle: one refactor (3089e771) made it forgeable. Durable content-addressed identity in docspec-rs is the replacement it should have waited for.
- **Wrong sequencing.** A documented docspec-rs plan to replace this SQLite/Python staging existed (ff21142f), yet these tickets polished the path it targeted. Within 3 days (55ba3479), the optimization work (gubr, 3djs, most of hnxb) became test-only or dormant. The correctness tickets (gesp, d2lk) survived.
- **Criteria that restate the fix.** Several acceptance criteria restate the implementation ("count one validation", "record bytes hashed") rather than an outcome such as elapsed time on real input. Closure was declared on fixture counts, and ct-u7gg found gaps the next day.

Out of scope, noted only: the VerifiedFile constructor weakness likely also defeats ct-1dsp's "forged proof must fail" guarantee (fork B).
