# November 10, 2026 (evening) — archive 77673bfb8fcf

The 34,826 GovInfo "Content Details" pages that were published as documents beside their hearings
are now folded into those hearings, so the archive holds 323,087 documents instead of 357,471.
Documents now take the official House file name when a copy has one, and their kind follows that
name. Thirty dead links that showed another document's recovered copy now show the right file. The
release also adds 392 documents from the capture of November 10, three new meetings and 25
nominations.

Published 2026-11-10 at 20:27:52Z, replacing release 619db5f9e08e (published earlier the same day).

## What changed

- **GovInfo details pages fold into their hearings (ct-m878).** A GovInfo "Content Details" page
  holds navigation, not hearing text, yet each was published as a document beside the hearing it
  describes. 34,826 of them (34,588 package pages and 238 part pages) are no longer documents.
  34,824 of their addresses are now listed as sources of the hearing they describe. Two pages attach
  to no hearing: CHRG-115hhrg24725 and CHRG-116hhrg49571 are multi-part hearings with no file for
  the whole package, so their package pages are no longer listed anywhere; the pages of their parts
  are placed on the parts. Every link key of a folded page shows a removal notice with its file name
  and last release; none redirects (owner decision: "Just fold in, no redirect needed") and none
  vanishes.
- **A help page is no longer a document (ct-aoxp).** The House Committee Repository's help page
  (`docs.house.gov/committee/Help.aspx`) was listed as a document of 81 meetings. It is removed;
  each of those meetings keeps at least one document, and the page's two link keys show a removal
  notice.
- **Official file names (ct-h8dr, ct-yuf0).** When any copy of a document carries the official House
  file name (for example `HHRG-117-PW00-Wstate-P000618-20210414.pdf`), that name now leads. 18,196
  documents change name: 18,031 take the official name, 154 whose House and committee names
  disagreed take the name chosen by reading the document, and 11 take the name of a copy fetched on
  November 10. No document's saved file changes because of its new name. Which listed address is
  marked as the saved copy moves with the name (36,687 marks).
- **Document kinds follow the official name (ct-h8dr, ct-isnv).** A document's kind is read from its
  leading file name, so the House's words now replace the committees' own: many documents filed as
  "testimony" become "witness-statement", and many amendments become "committee-amendment". 16,511
  documents change kind, and documents with no kind fall from 45,340 to 40,236; none loses every
  kind. `schema.json` now publishes the synonyms: its `document_kind` entry names
  "witness-statement" and "testimony" as one kind, and its note says to filter them together. Either
  word matched 63,354 documents before and matches 64,128 now. Other words return fewer documents;
  see Known problems. Broader document families barely move: biography 22,651 to 22,523, report
  3,086 to 2,965, amendment 19,833 to 21,054.
- **Titles (ct-ccsi, ct-h8dr).** 1,783 titles change: 1,166 documents gain a title, 501 lose one and
  116 get a different one. Junk link text no longer becomes a title: 103 titles of just "Biography"
  become "Biography - " followed by the person's name, and 499 documents titled with a bare label,
  number or page title now show their file name instead. 551 untitled documents take the link text
  of an identical copy, and 615 take the link text of a committee page fetched on November 10,
  mostly a witness's name or a bill, vote or amendment label. Two "H.R. ___" placeholder titles are
  removed.
- **Recovered copies of dead links corrected (ct-u9y4).** When a committee page is gone, the archive
  shows a recovered copy of its file. Some dead links had been matched to a copy with the same file
  name that holds a different document, taken from another committee's site or another hearing's
  folder (a Science Committee address shown with a copy from Ways and Means, for example). On an
  owner decision, 69 permanent rejection records withdraw those matches and 24 recovered files are
  re-recorded under their own addresses. A dead link now joins a recovered copy only when that copy
  is the file the link names. As a result (with two errors listed under Known problems) 25 documents
  come apart into 53, 24 documents are new, recovered-copy rows go from 12,632 to 12,587, and no
  file leaves the release. The links this moves are listed in the next section. This also settles
  the "Bowman Testimony.pdf" defect named in the previous release: link key `cae8e4ea34ec603a` again
  shows the 2012 Oversight testimony, and the 2018 Education and Workforce testimony is its own
  document.
- **Meetings marked held, and transcripts (ct-h8dr).** With official names showing, the archive now
  recognises a committee transcript by its House file name. 112 meetings each gain one committee
  transcript (99 already listed a printed hearing; 13 listed none), and the transcript's date equals
  the meeting date on all 112. 14 meetings move from outcome unknown to held on the strength of
  their transcript. Event 118323 (June 4, 2025), whose file the committee had named for April 9, was
  read: it is the printed volume of both hearings, so "held" is right.
- **Two new columns in the fetch log (ct-9j3z).** `url_state.parquet`, the archive's record of every
  address it fetches, gains `recheck_meeting_date` (the meeting date that sets how soon an address
  is fetched again) and `linked_from` (the captured pages that link an address still waiting to be
  fetched). They let the release gate trace a capture night's scheduling changes to the fetches that
  caused them. On this first night the gate's automatic check could not run, because the previous
  release has neither column, so the night's changes were checked by hand against the capture's own
  log: each of the 1,070 addresses fetched on November 10 has exactly one more attempt, on all 1,032
  capture addresses the new status and outcome equal the log, every next fetch is scheduled after
  the last, and a "last good" time moved only on a good fetch. Not checked: the band of days behind
  each next fetch.
- **Recovery requirements (ct-l7uh, ct-umtm).** 13 dead addresses now count as satisfied by a
  qualified recovered copy, so the capture stops queuing them again: the 12 Internet Archive
  (Wayback Machine) copies and the one Duke University copy imported on November 8 and 9. The pinned
  requirements file grows from 1 row to 14 (the 14th was already satisfied), which also changes the
  requirements hash in `capture_profile.json`. Two disclosures: 8 of the 12 Wayback copies were
  archived under a spelling variant of the dead address (`http` for `https`, port 80, a double slash
  or letter case), accepted on an owner decision; and the recovery decisions identify each Wayback
  copy by an address key the archive computes, not by an identifier the Internet Archive publishes.
- **New material from November 10.**
  - *Capture.* The scheduled capture ran from 14:03 to 14:22Z on a budget of 2,000 requests, with
    direct fetches only. It fetched 1,032 addresses; 5 returned "410 Gone" (budget.house.gov
    addresses that a page wrote wrongly). It adds 392 documents (197 files new to the archive's raw
    store, 195 files already stored there but new to the release), 610 new addresses on documents
    the archive already held, and the link text and listing page of 715 addresses. 318 newly found
    links join the queue of addresses not yet fetched, which goes from 41,887 to 41,175. Two
    documents gain a date from a second copy's file name. Two recovered documents that held the same
    file under two dead addresses become one original document, because the committee's own copy of
    that file was fetched.
  - *Meetings.* Congress.gov meeting records were read again: 35 records, 24 of them stored as new
    versions and 11 unchanged. The archive gains three meetings (events 338820, 338821 and 338822,
    dated November 16 to 19, 2026) and updates 32 others; one meeting's date moves. Meeting 119590
    gains 4 witnesses.
  - *Nominations.* 25 nominations are published for the first time, because a re-read meeting now

  - *Recordings.* 123 recordings are new. 118 are YouTube videos linked or embedded on committee

## Links that now show a different file

30 link keys and 38 lookup keys that showed the wrong document now show the right one. A link key is
the identifier in an archive link to a document; a lookup key is the identifier the archive uses to
find the document at a given address. The 30 link keys and 30 of the lookup keys belong to 30 dead
addresses; the other 8 lookup keys are second spellings of 8 of those addresses (an encoded space,
`%20`). Each had shown a recovered copy that holds a different document. The cause is the correction
above: 69 permanent rejection records withdraw the wrong matches, and 24 files are re-recorded under
their own addresses.

For 21 of the 30 addresses the key now shows a recovered copy of that address's own file. For the
other 9 it shows the official GovInfo hearing print, or House report, that an earlier accepted
record ties to the address as containing the statement, rather than the single statement file. For
seven of the eight prints, the witness list names the person; the 2008 print CHRG-110hhrg43278 has
no witness list in the archive, and its own text holds the prepared statement of Representative
Allen Boyd, who appeared as a Member. Not verified: that each print or report holds the statement in
full.

This is the first release to use named corrections in the release gate (ct-4nax). Before, the gate
refused any published key that answered a different file. Now it passes such a key only when the
reviewed cause record names that exact key with both files and the decision behind the change; an
unnamed key still stops the release. The gate checks that the record names exactly what changed, not
that the new file is the right document: the record makes that claim, and an independent review
re-checked all 68 against both releases. Proving each change from the published rejection records
instead of a hand-written list is ct-faan.

## Known problems in this release

Each is recorded in the cause record as an accepted defect with its ticket, or is a limit the
release checklist asked to state.

- **Exact-kind filters return fewer documents (ct-isnv).** A filter on one kind word, such as a
  saved site link with `?kind=amendment` or the Explorer's document search, now misses documents
  whose official name uses another word. "testimony" and "witness-statement" are published as one
  kind; the other words are not yet. Before and after: amendment 4,975 to 3,687; opening-statement
  5,324 to 4,693; statement 4,355 to 3,985; legislative-text 1,060 to 751; biography 1,288 to 1,042;
  committee-report 374 to 216; transcript 1,214 to 1,134. Filtering on the official word as well
  helps: amendment or committee-amendment matched 19,616 documents before and 20,847 now;
  opening-statement or member-statement 10,583 and 10,938.
- **A transcript under the wrong hearing (ct-fk4w).** Event 101019 (June 20, 2013) now lists
  `HHRG-113-WM02-Transcript-20130620.pdf`, which the committee had named `20130710HL.pdf`. It is the
  transcript of the July 10, 2013 hearing "The Delay of the Employer Mandate", which the House filed
  under the June 20 event. The event was already marked held by its own printed hearing, and that
  does not change.
- **One report shown as two documents, twice (ct-m3h3).** Two Ways and Means report PDFs
  (`hr3210rptlegtext.pdf` and `hr4954commrptpt1.pdf`) each exist as two files one byte apart with
  the same text. The recovered-copy rule cannot read PDF text, so each report now shows as two
  documents. Their 4 recovered copies now sit on the second document, and their 2 lookup keys lead
  to it; it still serves the old file.
- **Titles (ct-jcnd).** Two documents gain bare bill-number placeholders as titles, "H.R.___," and
  "H.Res.___", which the placeholder rule misses. 7 new titles start with "Download", 12 gained
  titles are a file name, and one title "H.R. 5430" becomes "BILLS-116hr5430rfs.pdf".
- **Some documents still hold several recovered files (ct-u9y4).** The correction settles the cases
  it names, not all. By the correction's own count, 149 documents still hold several recovered
  files: 31 with the same text, 25 whose text differs, and 93 scans and page captures not yet read.
- **At most 2,000 first schedules a night.** A capture night that schedules more than 2,000
  addresses for their first fetch with a meeting date now waits for a person instead of publishing
  on its own. The limit matches the capture's nightly budget of 2,000 requests, so an ordinary night
  passes and a catch-up run is reviewed; raising the budget means revisiting the limit.
- **Witness keys change when a meeting record is re-read (ct-uywf).** Re-reading a Congress.gov
  meeting record gives every witness on it a new key, even when nothing about the witness changed.
  This time 37 keys on 8 meetings changed. `witness_key_map.parquet` maps each old key to its new
  one.
- **New recordings cannot be traced to their page from the release's kept evidence (ct-zdj8).** Of
  the 118 new YouTube recordings with no meeting, 83 have no entry in the table of page links that
  the build keeps. Each video id was found by searching the raw text of the 232 pages first read on
  November 10, which shows the video is on a page fetched that day but not which link made the
  recording.

  storage suite). The gate change for named corrections passed the same suites and an independent
  review.
- **Proof build.** The same code on the previous release's inputs plus the correction gave release
  8aa4f840c41b. Its 43 difference groups were each reconciled by an independent model reviewer.
- **Release candidate.** The nightly built 77673bfb8fcf and stopped at the release gate with 78
  difference groups. Every change in the proof build reappears, and every change beyond it traces to
  the 1,094 new receipts, the archive's records of individual fetches and recoveries (1,032 from the
  capture, 38 from the meetings re-read and 24 from the correction). The reviewed cause record
  (`model/jobs/release-causes/619db5f9e08e-77673bfb8fcf.json`) explains 67 groups, accepts 11 as
  known defects and names the 68 corrections.
- **Independent review.** A separate reviewer re-derived every difference from the two releases with
  their own queries. They found no data defect, confirmed all 68 corrections, and asked for 7 groups
  to be relabelled as accepted defects and 9 statements to be corrected; the record was amended and
  the changes confirmed.
- **Rebuild.** Merging the gate change discarded the first candidate. The rebuild gave the same
  release id, and all 32 release files were byte-identical to the first candidate. The gate passed
  with the 68 named corrections, the release was published at 20:27:52Z, and the publisher's own
  check against the public site passed.
