# Body text: one read, one lake table, one engine export, indexed by SpicyEngine

Plan, 2026-11-08. Fills the `pdf_text` slot in
[engine-first-data-model.md](../engine-first-data-model.md) §6.2/§6.6 and
supersedes the deferred `search_index.sqlite` route (ct-m704). Nothing here is
built yet. Measurements behind every number live under
`.cache/archive-release/investigations/pdf-text-*-20261108/`, each with a
README; the third review round's export and join probes are in the session
scratchpad `pq-review/`.

## Facts

- 295,544 PDF bodies, 426 GB, in the locked raw bucket. 8,709 over 8 MiB hold
  61% of the bytes; 739 over 64 MiB hold 101 GB. Nothing over 4 MB has been
  sampled. 34,039 ZIP documents (184 GB) are GovInfo packages whose members
  are captured as their own bodies (`expansion = 'expanded'`), so their text
  arrives through the member bodies; 8,442 WARC documents (2.4 GB, mostly
  committee-site statements, disclosures, amendments and vote records) are
  envelopes that nothing unwraps today.
- Text-layer PDFs average 30 pages and 1,815 characters per page: about 8.9M
  pages and 16 GB of text. Textless PDFs are 14% of documents but 1.9% of
  pages; pages under 50 characters inside text-layer PDFs are another 10%.
  So OCR touches 2% to 12% of pages, on a size-biased sample.
- Native PyMuPDF text costs 8 ms/page. Apple Vision alone (accurate, 200 dpi,
  correction off, request reused) costs 0.19 s/page single-process and agrees
  with the native layer at 0.983 on text-layer pages; no scan has been judged
  against a reference. Docling wrappers were slower and its layout model drops
  footnotes. Tesseract 5.5.4 `tessdata_fast` matched Vision at 0.81 s/page on
  CPU. Vision parallelizes by process: 4.8× at 8 workers, measured with
  correction on.
- zstd-3 compresses the text 3.45× (zstd-12: 3.86× at 9× the CPU); dictionary
  encoding changes nothing. docspec-rs exports hardcode zstd-3 and cap row
  groups by rows only; the lake writer's 32 MiB byte cap does not apply to
  exports.
- SpicyEngine: one Parquet pin per table or up to 16 `joined` sisters on a
  same-named, same-typed key; text inline, 16 MiB per row counting the
  synthesized record; BM25 in SereneDB, full reindex per publish; one measured
  ratio of 2.2 bytes of index per byte of text, projecting about 35 GB. It
  runs on one Colima VM (100 GiB disk, 16 GiB memory) with no instance limits;
  blue/green holds three generations at a switch.

## 1. Extract

One producer on the readings framework (`lake/readings.py`: anti-join work
list, bounded workers, per-batch commits, retry once then `unreadable`, no
journal), with four parsers and a work list from sniff `format`:

| Parser | Bodies | Text derivation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pdf_text` | `pdf` | native page text with GPO normalization; Vision for pages under about 50 characters with images, or garbled (check whether spicy-docs' unmapped-glyph detection catches the shifted-font case before adding a dictionary test) |
| `html_text` | `html` | spicy-docs `body_text` markup reader, `<pre>` layout kept |
| `xml_text` | `xml` | spicy-docs `body_text` markup reader |
| `warc` | `warc` | spicy-docs `read_warc_response` unwraps the member and checks its payload digest; the payload is sniffed and routed to the derivation above |

The existing `html` and `xml` readings stay pinned and untouched; re-reading
10 GB of HTML and XML once is cheaper than a version bump there.

- **One stream per body, one open.** The page rule is one spicy-docs
  strategy, native with OCR fallback. The pass yields the text, `page_count`,
  `ocr_pages`, `text_sha256`, the §6.2 fingerprint over the native layer where
  it exists, and `text_head`.
- **OCR in spicy-docs:** `AppleVision` takes the pixmap, keeps one request per
  instance, exposes `usesLanguageCorrection`. **One engine per version:** the
  declared version names Vision; a host without Vision leaves OCR-needing
  bodies on the work list. A Tesseract backend (which spicy-docs lacks) would
  be its own version, built only if a portable pass is wanted.
- **Version** is a declared constant in `reading_versions.py` over the rule,
  thresholds and OCR settings (`docs/development.md`). PyMuPDF, the spicy-docs
  digest and the macOS build go in `reading-version-diagnostics.json`;
  `macos_build` is recorded on rows with OCR'd pages, null otherwise.
- **Framework changes named as work:** the worker returns text by file (the
  supervisor pipe caps output at 8 MiB, which the longest hearings exceed); a
  fixed document deadline plus an in-worker per-page deadline replaces the
  single 15 s pool timeout; `max_cache_bytes` near the 10 GiB ceiling with
  batches sized by bytes, so large-PDF batches do not end as `pending_reads`.
- **Bounds (§6):** `size_limit` 64 MiB (`MAX_STORED_BYTES`) on the first pass,
  raised later under a measured RSS bound; 1 GiB per worker
  (`WORKER_MEMORY_BYTES`), to be confirmed with Vision under `RLIMIT_AS`;
  `failed_pages` recorded, never a partial result passed as complete.
- **Census before the full run,** inside `ParserWorker`: native plus OCR over
  about 200 bodies stratified by size, including the over-8 MiB and
  over-64 MiB tail; correction-off throughput at 8 processes; fidelity on real
  scans against a reference (GovInfo HTML of the same hearings, or about 20
  hand-checked pages). Pass limits set in advance: OCR-page share, total
  hours, worker RSS, texts over 8 MiB. Verify the ZIP expansion count in the
  lake's `captures`.
- **Order:** newest Congress first; weekly new bodies at the head.

## 2. Save

- **`readings`** gets the narrow row per parser as §6.2 lists it (`status`,
  `error_type`, `page_count`, `text_bytes`, `text_head`, `fingerprint`,
  `content_date`). That is all the model reads.
- **`body_text`** (Iceberg, PK `(body_sha, parser, parser_version)`,
  partitioned like `readings`): full `text`, `page_offsets` list<int32>
  (UTF-8 byte offsets of page starts; one page for HTML, XML and WARC
  payloads that are pages), `ocr_pages` list<int32>, `text_sha256`,
  `macos_build`. Plain text, no markup. No truncation in the lake.
- **Commit order:** `body_text` first, then `readings`; the work list
  anti-joins both, and `append_rows`' anti-join makes a retry idempotent. A
  crash between the two commits therefore re-does one body, never loses one.
- **Export:** one docspec-rs SQL-job export of `body_text` per release:
  **one row per `body_sha` the release's `detail.saved_body` shows, at the
  declared version per parser** (no fingerprint deduplication: `same_text`
  groups only within one endpoint, so same-text copies on other sites keep
  their own `saved_body` and would lose their text; the shared-body case is
  already one row per sha); `Ranged` profile, ordered by `body_sha AS
  saved_body`, columns `saved_body, text, page_offsets, text_sha256`,
  **128 to 256 rows per row group** (a few thousand rows made a point read
  about 47 MB; 256 rows is about 4 MB with a 0.6 MB footer), truncation of
  text to Engine's bound in the export SQL only; create-only to a
  content-named key `derived/body_text/<sha256>.parquet`. Expected 4 to 6 GB.
  The export sorts the full rows with spill; its RSS and scratch are
  measured on the slice.
- **Pointer, not the release manifest.** §8.1 publishes nothing beyond the
  listed release files, the release ID hashes `files[]`, and the MCP's name
  rule and whole-file retain would reject or download it. The export gets its
  own `derived/body_text/current.json`: `sha256`, `bytes`, `rows`, footer
  digest, `body_text` snapshot, parser versions, coverage counts (bodies with
  text, OCR'd pages, `size_limit`, `unreadable`, WARC unwrapped). Engine pins
  the Parquet directly; the MCP reads the pointer.
- **Upload:** the release path's `create-file` is a single PUT reserving twice
  the file in memory, and a single PUT tops out near 5 GiB. Expose
  docspec-rs's multipart upload as a create-only CLI path before the export
  crosses that, or decision 3 becomes infeasible.
- **Lifetimes:** keep only the export the current pointer names; delete the
  rest (ARCH §8.6 rule applied to one generation). Each
  version bump adds a full copy of `body_text` until a rebuild; append-only,
  so rebuild is the compaction.
- **Not pinned in `publish-model.json`** until a model rule reads it
  (`content_date`, `same_text`); a pinned version needs an empty work list
  (§10.4), which forbids a partial newest-first backfill.
- **Explorer MCP** reads the pointer, caches the footer once per release,
  Range-reads one row group per body (min/max on `saved_body` prunes to one
  group; no page index needed), verifies the pointer's footer digest and
  `If-Match` on the ETag, then the row's `text_sha256`. The fetch reuses
  `body_reader`'s origin, no-redirect and deadline guards and parses outside
  the DuckDB worker, which keeps `enable_external_access` off. Live decoding
  stays the fallback for a body with no row. The site's `.gz` download of a
  WARC envelope is a separate, named gap: fixing it needs the model to publish
  the inner payload's type and a one-time create-only copy of each payload.

## 3. Index

Add the export to the documents source as `joined`, `on: saved_body`,
`columns: ["text"]`, `fields.text: ["text"]`. Engine must change first, in its
own repo:

- **Text out of the synthesized record** (view-rule bump; text hashed
  separately in the key check). Today the record is JSON of all columns and
  doubles as hit metadata, so text would count twice against the row limit,
  ship in every hit and record reply, and null out metadata on any hit over
  64 KiB.
- **Join direction.** DuckDB builds the hash table on the sister (fewer rows),
  so the whole text column lands on the build side, twice per publish (row
  pass and index build). Flip it so the narrow document rows build and the
  text probes; check row size on the sister alone; EXPLAIN it on SereneDB in
  the slice. §6.6: text stays off build sides.
- **Limits:** `memory_limit` per SereneDB instance; `statement_timeout` on
  serving sessions (the 30 s deadline is client-side only); fetch retry with
  resume and pruning of leaked `.fetch-*` files; stale version strings in
  PLAN.md and operations.md.
- **One generation, built in place** (decision 6). Engine's blue/green keeps
  a served, a rollback and a candidate generation plus their Parquet copies at
  the switch, about 3 × 35 GB + 18 GB for this corpus against 70 GB usable on
  the current VM. Locally no rollback is wanted and a publish window is
  acceptable, so Engine gains a single-instance mode: drop the served
  generation, build the new one, point at it. Peak is one index and one copy
  (about 41 GB projected), the full corpus is indexed with no Congress cap,
  and body search is unavailable for the build's duration (bounded by the
  2-hour gate) and until a rerun if a build fails. The zero-downtime mode
  stays in Engine for a hosted deployment with a disk to match.
- **Policy:** stack decision spicysearch 0010 (no body-content serving
  without a sealed policy) is listed as applying to Engine; the owner rules on
  these public bodies.

**Slice:** export the `0` and then the `0`+`1` prefixes from native text,
including the longest rows, and record the index slope, plus index bytes, publish wall time, peak
RSS per phase, spill and peak disk, export RSS and scratch, p50/p95 latency
(common words, rare words, phrase, recency sort, facet fan-out), a 20-hit
page of the longest bodies, `/v1/record` size and latency on the largest row,
`max(octet_length(to_json(text)))`, and the join plan. Go when: the projected
index plus one copy fits 70% of the disk; RSS slope under 50% of the
instance budget or spill bounded; publish under 2 hours; search p95 under
500 ms; longest-bodies page under 1 s; no row rule broken; record reply under
1 MB.

## 4. Delete

Once Engine serves body search: `text_index.py`, `index_builder.py`,
`passage_reader.py`, `archive_text_index.py`, the `text_index` manifest block
and `.cache/archive-release/text-index`. The per-body citation verifier stays,

## Decisions (owner, 2026-11-08)

1. Native text per page; OCR only where the layer is missing or garbled.
   (Docling on every page was chosen first and reversed on measurement.)
2. Apple Vision alone, accurate, correction off, 200 dpi, direct reused
   request. Reshaped by review: one engine per version; Tesseract fast would
   be a separate version, built only if a portable pass is wanted.
3. Increments by engine re-export to a content-named key; no Engine
   list-of-pins. Needs the multipart upload before the export passes 5 GiB.
4. `body_text` covers PDF, HTML and XML now, as new parsers of the new
   producer, plus WARC envelopes; captions later.
5. The MCP Range-reads the pinned export; no local copy. Reshaped by review:
   its own pointer file, per-row `text_sha256`, small row groups.
6. Engine stays on the current VM in a new single-instance mode: no
   rollback generation, build in place with a publish window, one index and
   one copy at peak, the full corpus indexed with no Congress cap. Hardware
   and the zero-downtime mode are for a hosted deployment later.

Simplified and then corrected by three review rounds: no custom Parquet
writer; no separate OCR-pages table (OCR for the corpus is hours); no per-line
boxes or page renders; no fingerprint deduplication (it would strand same-text
copies on other sites); truncation at export, never in the lake; the export is
not a release file.
